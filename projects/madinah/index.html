01 / The problem

Not another dashboard. An operating question.

When an operations centre handles reports around the clock, managers need to know what is late, what may become late, and where attention is most useful.

The idea came from my exposure to municipal operations monitoring. This independent project explores how the same decision questions can be structured through reporting and modelling. It does not use real municipal records and does not claim to describe the performance of Al Madinah Region Municipality.

  1. 1

    What is happening?

    Track open, closed, in-progress and overdue reports through defined KPIs and slices.

  2. 2

    Where is workload accumulating?

    Compare service categories, locations and team-capacity signals without implying causation.

  3. 3

    Which cases merit review?

    Test a predictive SLA-breach risk score, then account for false alerts and operational review capacity.

02 / The system

Make the analysis traceable.

I treated the work as a chain of evidence rather than a collection of visuals.

  1. 1

    Prepare and define

    Power Query shapes the synthetic reports; DAX makes KPI definitions usable in a four-page Power BI flow.

  2. 2

    Train and test

    Python and scikit-learn evaluate a risk classification model with time-ordered train, validation and test periods.

  3. 3

    Interpret and challenge

    Check point-in-time feature availability, label maturity, threshold choice and what an alert implies for a human reviewer.

03 / Model evaluation

Better recall.
More noise.

A technical re-evaluation removed four capacity features whose availability at report creation could not be verified and corrected 645 outcome labels not available at their original evaluation cutoffs. The review threshold was selected on validation data.

The comparison holds the same 6,057 resolved test cases. It measures discrimination and alert trade-offs on synthetic data, not field performance.

04 / Judgment & limits

What I would not claim.

A risk score can support prioritization. It cannot decide staffing, establish causation, or validate itself.

No real-world impact claim

This is an independent portfolio prototype on synthetic data. It has not reduced real SLA breaches or been adopted by a public agency.

No automatic escalation

With precision near one-third, many alerts would be false positives. Teams would need a review queue and workload limit before use.

No geographic certainty

Any mapped relationships need verified geography and source metadata. Feature importance is association, not explanation or causation.

Next: review and merge the corrected evaluation after Power BI Desktop verification; test thresholds against review capacity; document reliability by service category and area; and validate proposed actions with operations stakeholders.

The takeaway

Useful intelligence starts with honest trade-offs.

My contribution sits between operational understanding, analytical implementation and model review: defining the decision, building the reporting flow, and questioning whether a predictive signal is actually usable.
