The queue
is a question.

In a 24/7 municipal operations setting, seeing open and overdue reports is only a starting point. The real question is where a team should direct its next review.

This independent project grew from my exposure to municipal operations monitoring. It uses synthetic data to explore service-level reporting, workload patterns and SLA-breach risk—not real records or performance claims about Al Madinah Region Municipality.

Behind each alert.

A risk score matters only if the person receiving it can make a better decision.

I connected Power Query and DAX reporting to a Python and scikit-learn evaluation workflow. The predictive model was checked with time-ordered train, validation and test periods, then challenged for feature availability, label maturity and review workload.

See what happened

Four Power BI views track open, closed, in-progress and overdue reports; KPI definitions turn raw states into consistent reporting.

Find pressure

Service categories, location breakdowns and team-capacity signals reveal where synthetic workload accumulates, without implying causation.

Review what comes next

SLA-breach probabilities are triage signals. Model thresholds must be tested against the human cost of false alerts.

More noise.

A technical re-evaluation removed four capacity features whose availability at report creation could not be verified, and corrected 645 outcome labels unavailable at their original evaluation cutoffs.

Both versions are compared on the same 6,057 resolved synthetic test reports; the revised review threshold was chosen on validation data.

What I would
not claim.

No live impact

This is an independent prototype on synthetic data. It has not reduced real SLA breaches and is not an official municipal system.

No automatic decisions

With revised precision at 33.6%, a team would face substantial false alerts. Human review capacity must shape any alert policy.

No causal map

Geographic patterns need verified coordinates and metadata. Feature importance does not prove a cause, and original dashboards are not revised evaluations.

The next defensible step: Verify the remediation in Power BI Desktop, review and merge PR #1, then test thresholds against workload capacity and assess error rates by service category and area.
